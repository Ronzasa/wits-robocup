# v7.0: public-physics planning and receiver-aware cutbacks

Version: `7.0-hybrid-physics-planner`.

The user confirmed that v6.1 was uploaded to the course system and still did
not improve the league standing. That is distinct from pushing a GitHub branch.
This release targets match points and hard matchups rather than presenting a
lower concession count as proof of stronger overall play.

## What changed

- Dense ball forecasts and an acceleration/momentum reach model replace the
  sparse interception race. Reach constants are cached per field profile.
- The carrier compares receiver-led passes at 14, 20, and 26 units/s, reachable
  goal targets, and the existing recoverable touches. Candidates include the
  carrier's first physical contact with the ball and its achievable kick impulse.
- Short-term body obstruction is checked even when an opponent cannot kick.
- In the attacking third, an end-wall pass can rebound outside the goal mouth
  into a named receiver's path. The full route and post-bounce arrival race must
  qualify. This complements the retained side-wall outlets.
- The established cover role, balanced counterpress, quick counters, marking,
  keeper restrictions, and restart guards remain available.

These are bounded public-physics approximations, not access to hidden engine
state or a guarantee about opponent actions. No seed, opponent identity,
external service, or altered player attributes selects tactics at runtime.

Reproduced v6.1 losses showed intended shots whose first body contact changed
their direction, including backward rebounds. A simple clearance reorder failed
(143 → 130 screen points), so it was rejected. The full planner/interception
combination performed better than either component alone.

## The supplied real-life tactics

The supplied document's useful principles were width, triangles, coordinated
pressing, rotations, and cover behind attacks. Its “wall passes” mean teammate
one-twos, distinct from this simulator's solid-wall rebounds. All players have
the same course attributes; there is no stamina model or offside rule.

On the same 64-fixture screen, the strongest core scored 169 points. Adding a
settled diamond scored 152; give-and-go runs scored 146; combining them scored
144. These movement rules were therefore left out. Their labels were not used
as evidence that they would improve a simulator team. Cover and selective paired
pressing were already supported and remain in the release.

## Validation and reliability

Nine focused public-physics tests passed, covering shot reach under drag,
own-body shot reversal, legal impulse limits, named receiver collection,
cooldown body blocking, mirrored links, momentum-dependent interception,
and mirrored end-wall cutbacks with first-leg obstruction. The earlier cover,
marking, keeper, and side-wall regression suite also passed.

Official five-seed validation completed 2,000 decisions: mean 0.174 ms,
slowest 2.195 ms, against a 20 ms deadline. There were no exceptions,
overruns, missing actions, or non-finite values.
